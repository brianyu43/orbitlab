# OrbitLab Research Memo: How far does the benefit of correct symmetry extend?

2026-09-23 · Synthetic figures·object editing·control experiment of dynamics. This is a note for discussing the current results and is not an assertion of the confirmation of new high-performance models or the originality of the paper.

**Question.** How much do the constraints that ensure the rotation of input and output are consistent help meaningful generation, intervention, and future prediction? We distinguished good symmetry metrics from actual task success and compared where failure arises—from insufficient observation information, from perception, or from state transfer.

**Method.** In a separate workspace that preserved the original 15th-round data, we performed evaluation metrics such as audio comparison and baseline correction, flow comparison across the same expressions, learning comparison across the same number of iterations and times, repeated independent data sampling, and decoder comparison. Object editing separated the ground truth states from RGB inputs, while motion prediction involved replacing ground truth information, retraining on missing environmental information, comparing observation chapters 1, 2, 4, and 8, and future steps 1 through 61, as well as comparing opposite behaviors. The single model integrated all experiments.

**Newly verified boundaries.** At the same final time point, the speed estimation error for measurement+MLP input was 0.7405·0.0471·0.0574·0.0611 pixels/frame for each of the video frames 1·2·4·8. Although the benefit of adding motion viewing information is large, it did not continuously improve as the observation length increased. Eight of the frames also failed to resolve the instability of the long future. This measurement device leverages prior knowledge of circular shape, color, and physical properties. Even though it is the same video and behavior, it has also verified examples where the future is different due to hidden environments, and it does not extend the lower limit of this example to the overall test error limit.

**External verification and proof.** I divided the official SVIB public example of 500 pairs and learned 24 small models from scratch. C4 is better than the general models, but the average alpha of all four learning models was larger than the original copy, resulting in a total pixel error. The shared external test has 100 images, and some scenes do not actually change, so both the change area and the unchanged area were evaluated separately. This is not a reproduction of the entire official SVIB or the existing OrbitLab checkpoint transfer.

**Position as a research.** We identified problems overlapping with Slot Attention, ISA, SysBinder, Dreamweaver, SEN, Aether, PEnGUiN, PE-RL, and Sparse World Models. The claim that it is the first proposal of 'symmetry + hidden force' or a new high-performance structure is not appropriate. The current contribution candidate is a diagnosis that simultaneously controls the level of information provision, the validity of symmetry, the recognition stage, and the prediction time, and links them to success indicators and the reproducibility of failures. It is necessary to make a separate research judgment whether KAIST MLML will be interested in it or whether it is sufficient as a paper.

**Next research proposal.** In contrast to the experiments in the current plan, we propose to (1) verify long-term stability by training only collision residuals on the known-physics reference, (2) verify the prediction of the change zone beyond original copying in the fixed external split, and (3) directly compare one of the Aether or partial symmetry methods using the same input information and budget. The performance goals and budget must be established first before designing the verification experiments, and these three additional experiments have not yet been executed.

**Verification and remaining limitations.** The original 1,324 files match the initial hash. Predictive playback, aggregation, tables, and drawings were each tested. The repeated data consists of only three sets, and some object recognition experiments involve only one encoder initialization. The actual video, natural language instructions, and overall causal inference abilities were not verified. According to user requests, the original review sample of 240 pages was categorized into 9 human and 231 AI samples. The material verified by humans is not the entire set, and it differs from the sample from the three independent data verification experiments.
