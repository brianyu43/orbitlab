#Overlap of prior research of OrbitLab and scope of claims

2026-09-23. To compare the implementation of the original five methods in terms of text and core implementation, four studies that directly address external forces, partial symmetry, and domain prediction were added. We completed a comparative record of the specified close methods, but this does not mean that we have comprehensively reviewed all literature or demonstrated the originality of the research. We read the original text and the public code, but did not execute the entire training of other methods.

Currently, OrbitLab is more reasonable described as a set of control experiments that divide the benefits of symmetry constraints into input information, perception errors, and prediction time. The explanation that it first proposed object-level representations, force estimation, partial symmetry, and manipulation based on commands is unsupported. A new high-performance world model has also not yet been proven.

## The closest method and the actual difference

| Method | Key points confirmed from the original | Points of overlap and differences with OrbitLab |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slot Attention | By sharing and updating attention with competition, RGB features are assigned to objects' slots. | Slot separation and shared decoder are the existing methods. Our RGB experiment is a reduction implementation and a diagnosis of other losses, not a reproduction of the original author's performance. |
| Invariant Slot Attention | Provides coordinates relative to object position, size, and direction to the attention and decoder. | Handling geometric symmetry between the object and itself is not new. We must distinguish between the estimated direction of each object and the precise global C4 interaction guarantees. |
| SysBinder | Splits the internal of the object slot into factor blocks and uses block-level updates and prototype memory. | Object separation and property separation are separate concepts. Independent concept manipulation cannot be claimed solely based on high probe scores. |
| Dreamweaver | Maintains recurrent block-slots in videos to predict future tokens and change combinations. | Recombination of objects, properties, and motion is the existing goal. When identifying the block to edit, we must compare the answer-based analysis used when applying it and the input from expression learning. |
| SEN | Send video observation as a representation with known military operations and learn equivariant transitions. | Inserting symmetric representations into the world model is itself a conventional method. Currently, we cannot directly compare the pixel generation and state supervision loss and metrics of OrbitLab. |
| Aether, NeurIPS 2023 | Estimates the global external force field from observation trajectories and combines it with object interactions in local coordinates. The field that varies across input channels is conditioned on the latent summary and FiLM. | **The combination of hidden external force and equivariant dynamics directly overlaps.** We controlled the provision/estimation/missing of simple constant sum/resistance and the RGB observation length. There is no evidence that it is superior in general spatial external force learning. |
| PEnGUiN, 2025 | Equivariant·Combines general GNN paths into a symmetry score dependent on the input and classifies partial symmetry. | There is also an argument that it "softens according to the degree to which symmetry is satisfied." OrbitLab's fixed comparison model did not reproduce this input-specific combined structure and did not evaluate MARL policy performance. |
| PE-RL, arXiv v2 / ICLR 2026 | Due to equivariant·general one-step prediction mismatch, it detects local symmetry violations and selects Bellman updates and policies. | The error between incorrect symmetry and the strategy to avoid it selectively overlap. OrbitLab measured errors in video inference·autonomous rollout, but there are no results for value function·policy optimization. |
| Modeling What Changes, arXiv v1, 2026-09-02 | Predict changes gate and residuals in structured object states and copy the rest. Calculate the scores of the actual changed object and the unchanged object. | Evaluation that splits the cause into non-target preservation/unchanged contrast/oracle is directly related. The paper also explicitly states the limitations of the changed object's motion prediction and long rollout. It should not claim that our RGB recognition bottleneck and the success of structured states are combined. |

The reference numbers and implementation records for the five methods above are in [First Comparison Record](NEAREST_METHODS_REVIEW_EN.md). The additional four methods are linked to the following sources and implementation verification.

## Direct overlap with external force and symmetry

§3.1–3.3 of Aether deals with cases where the influence of a fixed global field and relative object interactions are observed together. It adds the estimated force to a vector state and converts it to local coordinates. The dynamic field in §3.2 uses the potential summary of the input-dependent field. Therefore, one cannot claim OrbitLab's originality solely by converting the force into a vector or by estimating the force in the observed motion. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20679) · [Official implementation](https://github.com/mkofinas/aether)

We confirmed that the `f88cac1611d9fd8400d0ce8ae4333c936cf09022` and `AetherLocalizer.canonicalize_inputs` of the fixed commit `create_edge_attr` rotate speed and power together. `Aether.forward` attaches the output power of the field network to the state and sends it to the GNN. The separate state-to-state implementation of `DynamicFieldAether` uses `GraphSummary` and `FilmedNetwork`. We did not assume that this state-to-state reference is the same as all sequence models and experimental settings in the paper.

Section 4–6 of PEnGUiN presents the combination of general/symmetric paths and score estimation per input, and compares asymmetric conditions such as sensor bias, safety zones, and target relevance. The text verified is arXiv v1. Although the same method was confirmed for publication on the official academic page, the search and the text do not indicate that code review was performed because the author's official implementation address could not be found. [Text](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15615v1) · [RLJ publication record](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2025/papers/Paper310.html)

Section 4–6 of PE-RL v2 distinguishes gates based on Bellman errors caused by local symmetry violations and predictor mismatches. The theoretical framework presented in this paper pertains to the MDP, reward, and gate conditions specified, and does not guarantee that physics can be identified from imperfect RGB observations. The implementation link for the author's project was `Code (Coming Soon)` at the time of review. This research did not reproduce the implementation or author performance. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00915v2) · [Author Project](https://pranaboy72.github.io/perl_page/)

## Intersection of evaluation that divides change and preservation

Modeling What Changes §III, §IV, §IX and Appendix F were reviewed. The object state and action are input, and the gate is supervised by the correct change mask. The original paper also explains the difference between structured states and pixel recognition, the limitations of change object prediction compared to no-op, and the limitations of some contrast groups and evaluation definitions. This paper cannot be described as a relative improvement in dense baseline by transferring the completed physical prediction ability. [Original v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02046v1)

The default branch in the official repository was `master`, and the commit `69d559263dbd5a1aaac57697471c8517fdeb00d5` was fixed and read. We verified the gate×delta for `SparseResidualHead.forward` and the answer-change mask supervision for `sparse_residual_loss`, confirming the predicted-gate / true-gate / no-op separation in oracle diagnosis. The current code also includes probabilistic residual head and additional split options, so it did not match the experiments in paper v1 or all functionalities of the current repository. The data problem numbers listed in the repository comments are not re-verified using our original data, so they were not used to support the comparison performance claims. [Official code](https://github.com/ParamThakkar123/sparse_world_models)

## What can you argue

| Claim candidate | Current judgment | Basis or evidence still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First combined symmetry, objects, external forces, and operations | Not supported | It overlaps broadly with the above directly related research. It does not generate originality by listing elements. |
| Joint rotation constraints provide an advantage in prediction at a single step | Currently, the simulator's verified empirical results | A gain was observed compared to general interaction across three sets of independent data. There is no result indicating superiority over the known physical reference. |
| The superiority of the structure changes when there is no environmental information | Verified diagnosis under current conditions | Completed function·response weighting tests comparable to the comparison of 108 conditions that concealed cooperation. It is not the optimal error law under arbitrary environmental conditions. |
| Longer observation distinguishes information deficiency and learning failure | Counting and interpretation are underway | We conducted an experiment with 1/2/4/8 chapters at the same final time point, action, and future. The effect is not confirmed before the entire playback and pair comparison are completed. |
| Generalizes the external transformation rules using only symmetry | Not supported in this reduction experiment | The C4 of SVIB preview was generally better than the general model, but both alpha averages had a larger overall error than identity. |
| Consolidated observation information × correct symmetry × failure boundaries of prediction length to be reproduced | Central candidate of the final research memo | The comprehensive observation length and the overall picture/reproduction bundle remain. It is not a separate new architecture or proof of global originality. |

If we choose to improve methods to the level of a follow-up paper, we must compare predictions that maintain multiple futures from partial observations using an Aether-type field path and a PEnGUiN-type input-specific mixture **using the same input information and computational budget**. This is the next research proposal to be selected based on the results of the current plan, and it is not the result of replication or achievement in this execution. Before immediately extending a model that failed on external examples to a large dataset, we must first examine the gap between the training data and holdout data, as well as the loss in the change zone and preservation zone.

## Scope of review and source preservation

The current review is limited to the nine methods mentioned above, which are directly related to object representation, geometric bias, hidden external forces, partial symmetry, and change prediction. Since we have also confirmed the existence of adjacent literature such as partial observation RL, we do not make the claim that “the combination of partial observation and symmetry is the first.” We did not infer academic interest or the adoption and support of a specific research laboratory.

We recorded the URLs, SHA-256, and commit hashes of the fixed code, as well as failed lookups, in `references/nearest_methods_v2/manifest.json`. The PE-RL source/metadata for Hugging Face returned a 404 error, indicating that the arXiv original paper was used with a specified version. The `main` lookup for Sparse World Models also returned a 404 error, but we verified `master` in the repository metadata before reading the actual code. The original paper and code are reference materials, and they are not bundled with the new reproduction distribution without permission.
