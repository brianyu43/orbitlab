# OrbitLab Generation Success Recovery: Confirmation on New Data

**Conclusion:** Increasing latent-flow training from 4,000 to 16,000 steps improved the automatic strict-pass rate for every initialization across three new data seeds. However, strictly successful images underrepresented small shapes, and some conditions produced too few successful samples. The prespecified **final milestone was therefore not met**. The experiment and verification are complete; this should not be read as completion of a product-quality generator.

## What was compared

Development results selected only the 4,000- and 16,000-step versions of the F0 conditional C4 latent flow for final comparison. The two settings share **the same AE, logit-mean decoder, flow initialization, data, optimizer, and 4,000-step checkpoint**. The candidate receives 12,000 additional training steps from that checkpoint. Both use Euler with 64 steps, guidance 1.0, and identical conditions and noise at generation time. Flow training duration is the difference. Models and evaluation criteria are recorded in the [pre-evaluation lock](../../../../generation_recovery/confirmation_v1/lock.json).

The confirmation data comprise independent synthetic scenes from seeds 770201, 770202, and 770203, with three freshly trained initializations per seed. Each model generated 20 × 128 = 2,560 samples for seen combinations and 4 × 128 = 512 for combinations excluded from training, or 3,072 in total. Both training durations together yielded 18 model paths. The [data manifest](../../../../generation_recovery/confirmation_v1/data/manifest.json) records 33,024 rotation orbits of base scenes with no overlap with previous data. Nevertheless, all three seeds came from the same synthetic generator, so this is not an independent test of real-world generalization.

## Why the final milestone failed

For the candidate, **all generated images** passed the center, size, and orientation diversity criteria in each seed. Considering **strictly successful images only**, size-distribution total variation (TV) was 0.283, 0.274, and 0.256 by seed, above the prespecified maximum of 0.20. For shape 2/color 0, only 11, 7, and 15 of the 384 images generated by the three initializations in each seed passed strictly. Each is below the minimum of 32 needed to judge per-condition diversity. Successful images thus concentrate at certain sizes. The model does not yet succeed across the full size distribution of the original data generator.

Among shape-2 generations that the reader estimated as small, strict success was 2.45%; among those estimated as large, it was 15.18%. These bins use **size estimated from generated images**, so they do not guarantee the true size of malformed images. They do support a follow-up hypothesis that small outlines are especially weak in shape and clarity. Red shape 2 had only about 2.9% strict success across all nine paths. Color accuracy itself was high, so the shortfall is mainly in shape and the strict outline criterion. ## Time, verification, and limitations

With four CPU threads, total training across the nine paths took about 72 minutes for AEs, 25 minutes for decoders, one minute for the initial 4,000-step flows, and three minutes for the additional 12,000 flow steps. Extension averaged about 20 seconds per path. Both generation settings required the same 256 raw C4 velocity evaluations per sample. For this small synthetic model, the experiment thus supports **substantial improvement per unit cost from additional flow training**. It does not establish the same result for other tasks.

The [recomputation check](../../../../generation_recovery/confirmation_v1/evaluation/verification.json) verified checkpoint, source, and noise hashes for all 18 results; replayed the first eight generated latents; and checked row-level metrics and diversity aggregation. The [pre-evaluation training audit](../../../../generation_recovery/confirmation_v1/pre_evaluation_training_audit.json) checked nine AEs/decoders and the 4,000- and 16,000-step checkpoints. The original execution wrapper omitted one provenance field for the extra training steps and raised a `KeyError` on the first path. Without changing model state or evaluation code, we added only that field, locked the [execution amendment record](../../../../generation_recovery/confirmation_v1/execution_amendment_01.json) before confirmation evaluation, and resumed from the existing checkpoints. This was not a fix made after viewing test/OOD scores.

Further research should use a new protocol with separate development and confirmation seeds, rather than reselecting this candidate on the present confirmation results. A reasonable first step is to analyze shape-2 and small-size failures with images, latents, and outline measurements, then test one change at a time, such as size-balanced sampling or a spatially structured representation. The fixed evaluation criteria should remain, and diversity among successful samples must be an explicit goal. Human judgment results and performance on other shapes or real images are still unavailable.
